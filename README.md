# EU AI Act: Regulatory Response to Unsanctioned Agent Behaviour

> **Project developed during the Apart Research Hackathon**

## 📌 Overview
As advanced AI systems are increasingly evaluated in environments involving agentic orchestration, tool use, and multi-agent interaction, the risk of unsanctioned or rogue agent behavior grows. 

This repository contains a structured policy and regulatory-drafting research project designed to address these risks. We developed a simulated **Request for Information (RFI)** under **Article 91(1) of the EU AI Act (Regulation EU 2024/1689)**. The framework provides a proportionate mechanism for the European AI Office to investigate publicly reported unsanctioned agent behavior during cyber testing (simulating the July 2026 Hugging Face incident).

## 🎯 Key Contributions
Our framework translates complex regulatory requirements into a structured, evidence-oriented investigative tool. Key features include:

- **Traceable Legal Framework:** Connects scope, systemic-risk assessments, evaluation conditions, and incident-reporting directly to specific provisions of the EU AI Act.
- **19-Point Evidence Inquiry:** Organizes the investigation around verifiable records, including incident timelines, model configurations, evaluation environments, and containment measures.
- **Secure Evidence Model (Annex II):** A strict production protocol that protects dual-use-sensitive cybersecurity material, confidential business information, and personal data while preserving the regulator's ability to evaluate compliance.

## 📂 Repository Contents
- `RFI_FINAL_Regulatory_Response.pdf`: The main research report detailing the legal basis, methods, public-information context, and the complete regulatory framework.
- `RFI_FINAL_EU_AI_Office.pdf`: The actual simulated artifact (The Draft Request for Information document) directed to the AI provider.

## 🛡️ Relevance to AI Safety & Alignment
This project explores the intersection of technical AI alignment and governance. By establishing robust protocols for investigating rogue AI actions (such as bypassing evaluation safeguards or unauthorized external system interactions), this framework contributes to the broader effort of ensuring advanced AI models remain interpretable, controllable, and accountable under real-world regulatory constraints.
